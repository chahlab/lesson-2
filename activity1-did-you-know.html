Fact 1: Honey never spoils.

Explanation of the Fact:

Honey possesses unique chemical properties that prevent the growth of bacteria and other microorganisms that typically cause food to decay. It has very low moisture content, which draws water out of bacteria, effectively dehydrating and destroying them. Additionally, honey is highly acidic, with a pH level between 3.2 and 4.5, and bees introduce an enzyme called glucose oxidase into the nectar, which produces hydrogen peroxide. This combination of low moisture, high acidity, and natural antibacterial agents allows honey to remain perfectly preserved for thousands of years, as evidenced by archaeologists finding edible pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs.

How does the moisture level in honey prevent bacterial growth?

Answer:

This question focuses on the mechanism of osmosis in food preservation. Bacteria require water to survive, reproduce, and metabolicly function. Because honey is a highly concentrated sugar solution with very little water, it acts as a hypertonic environment. When a bacterial cell comes into contact with honey, water is drawn out of the bacterium and into the honey to balance the solute concentrations. This process of osmosis deprives the bacteria of the hydration they need to survive, rendering them inactive and ensuring the honey remains spoiled-free.

Fact 2: Bananas are botanically classified as berries, but strawberries are not.

Explanation of the Fact:

In botanical terminology, a berry is a fleshy fruit that originates from a flower with a single ovary and typically contains multiple seeds embedded in the flesh. Bananas meet all of these structural requirements because they develop from a single flower ovary and have a soft middle layer. On the other hand, strawberries develop from a flower with multiple ovaries, and the sweet red flesh is actually enlarged receptacle tissue rather than a mature ovary. The actual fruits of a strawberry are the tiny, seed-like specks on the outside, which are botanically classified as "achenes."

Why are the tiny specks on the outside of a strawberry considered the true fruit?

Answer:

This question addresses the specific botanical definition of a fruit, which must develop directly from the ovary of a flower. In a strawberry flower, each individual ovary is distinct and sits on a central core called the receptacle. When fertilization occurs, each of these tiny ovaries ripens into a separate, dry fruit called an achene, which encloses a single seed. Because these achenes are the direct result of the ripened ovaries, botanists classify them as the true fruits, while the large, red, juicy part we eat is simply the swollen base of the flower holding them together.

Fact 3: Sound travels roughly four times faster in water than it does in air.

Explanation of the Fact:

The speed of a sound wave depends heavily on the density and elasticity of the medium through which it travels. While water is denser than air, it is also significantly less compressible and much more elastic, meaning its particles bounce back into place quickly after being displaced. Because the molecules in water are packed much closer together than the widely dispersed molecules in air, they can transmit the mechanical vibrations of sound waves from one molecule to the next with much greater efficiency and speed.

Why does the closeness of molecules allow sound to travel faster?

Answer:

This question explores the kinetic transmission of mechanical waves through matter. Sound is a longitudinal wave that propagates by causing particles to collide with neighboring particles, passing energy down a line. In a gas like air, molecules are far apart, meaning a displaced molecule must travel a relatively long distance before bumping into another one to pass the vibration along. In a liquid like water, the tight proximity of the molecules means they bump into each other almost instantly, drastically reducing the delay between particle collisions and accelerating the overall speed of the wave.
