Black holes are regions of space where gravity is so strong that even light cannot escape. The boundary around a black hole is called the event horizon.

using the < strong > tag

A black hole does not simply “suck” everything around it. Objects can orbit a black hole just as planets orbit a star, as long as they remain at a safe distance. The strongest gravitational effects occur very close to the black hole.

using the < i > tag

Scientists cannot directly see a black hole because it does not produce or reflect light. Instead, they can observe how it affects nearby objects, such as stars and gas.

using the < mark > tag

Some black holes are surrounded by a disk of extremely hot material called an accretion disk. This material can become so hot that it releases enormous amounts of X-rays.

using the < small > tag

Black holes can have very different masses. Some are only a few times more massive than the Sun, while supermassive black holes can contain the mass of millions or even billions of Suns.[these values are approximate]

using the < del > tag

Black holes were once considered purely theoretical objects. The idea was difficult to test, but scientists eventually found strong evidence for their existence. An important milestone came when the first image of a black hole’s shadow was released in 2019.

using the < sub > tag

Scientists use symbols and equations to describe black holes. For example, the chemical formula for water is H2O, where the 2 is written as a subscript.

using the < sup > tag

Astronomers also use powers of ten when describing extremely large distances and masses. For example, 103 means 10 multiplied by itself three times, giving 1,000.
